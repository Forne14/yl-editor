A field left open when another editor's change arrives

Issue #48. Today the chrome can save a half-typed value over another editor's change without saying so. This page shows three ways to stop that. Pick one; options A and B need new copy, option C reuses the conflict toast that already ships.

What happens today

  1. Amira is typing a new role for “Sam Lee” on the team page. The field is still open.
  2. Meanwhile Ben has changed the same role to “Head of design” and saved. Amira presses Discard (or Restore, or Unpublish) on the team page.
  3. Her action conflicts, so the chrome catches up: it takes Ben's version of the page and redraws the team list. Amira's open field is replaced, so the chrome closes it and saves what she had typed.
  4. Because the chrome has just taken Ben's version, that save goes through without a conflict. Ben's “Head of design” is gone, and neither of them is told.

The same thing happens without a list. A field that stays open through the catch-up keeps Amira's text, and when she leaves it, it saves over Ben's change. The swap only closes it sooner. Whichever option you choose applies to both cases.

Each option applies only if Ben's version changed this field. If his change was elsewhere on the page, Amira's text saves as normal, as it does today.

A · Keep her text as a failed save

The field keeps Amira's text with the red “failed” outline. The error toast offers Retry, which sends her text and overwrites Ben's change. Reopening the field starts from her text, as for any failed save. Needs one new string for the reason.

Light new copy

Not saved: someone else changed this field while you were editing it.

Retry is the only way out besides typing again. Ben's “Head of design” is not on screen anywhere.

B · Drop her text and say so

The field shows Ben's “Head of design”. An info toast says Amira's text was not saved. It hides itself like every info toast. Nothing to retry; her text is lost unless she remembers it. Needs one new string.

Light new copy

Your change to this field wasn’t saved: someone else changed it while you were editing.

Sam Lee

Head of design

Safest for Ben. Amira loses what she typed.

Dark new copy

Your change to this field wasn’t saved: someone else changed it while you were editing.

Sam Lee

Head of design

C · Treat it as a conflict on that field recommended

Exactly what happens today when a field's save meets another editor's change: the field keeps Amira's text with the red outline, and the conflict toast offers “Keep mine (overwrite)” or “Take theirs (reload field)”. Take theirs shows Ben's “Head of design”. No new copy and no new behaviour to learn.

Light existing copy

Someone else changed this field since you opened the page.

Sam Lee

Design lead, brand and

Both editors' work survives until Amira chooses. The copy says “since you opened the page”, which is still true here.
